# The human playbook

Everything in this file is work an agent cannot do. Not "should not" — cannot. It
requires a phone, a camera, a face, a bank account, or a relationship.

For a local business this file contains more of the value than all the others
combined, because the profile and the reviews outweigh the website in the ranking
system that decides whether the phone rings.

Hand this to the owner. It is written to be read by someone who does not work in
marketing.

---

## Week one: the things that must exist

**Google Business Profile. Two hours, then wait for the postcard.**

Go to google.com/business and search for your business first, because a profile
may already exist that someone else created or that Google generated
automatically. If it does, claim it rather than making a second one. Duplicate
profiles compete with each other and both lose.

Verification takes days to weeks, usually by postcard, sometimes by video call.
Start it now; everything else waits on it.

While you wait, prepare:

- **The exact name.** Exactly as it appears on your invoices. Do not add your
  city, or "cheap", or your services to it. That gets profiles suspended, and
  your competitors will report you.
- **The address decision.** If customers can come to you, publish the address. If
  you go to them, hide the address and set a service area instead. Do not use a
  home address you do not want public, and never use a mailbox service.
- **The main category.** This is the most important field on the whole profile.
  Pick the most specific one that matches your main work. Not "Contractor" if
  "Electrician" fits. Then add up to nine secondary categories for the other
  things you really do.
- **Hours.** Real ones. Being open when someone searches is now one of the top
  five things that decides whether you appear.
- **A phone number a human answers.** The same one that is on your website.
- **A description**, 750 characters. What you do, where, since when. Not a slogan.

**Twenty photographs. One afternoon.**

Take them yourself with a phone. Do not buy stock images: Google can identify
them and customers can tell.

What to shoot:

- Five finished jobs, wide enough to see the room.
- Five during the work, with people in them. These get the most views.
- Three of the team, faces visible.
- Two of the van or the equipment.
- Three details that show quality: a clean joint, a straight run, a proper finish.
- Two of the office or workshop if you have one.

Ignore anyone who tells you to add GPS data to the photos. Google strips it on
upload and it has never affected rankings.

**The name, address and phone block. Twenty minutes, once.**

Write it once, in a note on your phone, and copy-paste it from now on. Never
retype it, because you will type it differently and each variation makes you look
like a different business.

```
Name:    [exact name from your invoices]
Address: [street, number, postcode, city, province, country]
Phone:   [+39 …]
Website: [https://…]
```

## Week two: the first reviews

Reviews are between 16% and 20% of what decides local rankings, and they are the
first thing a customer reads. Ten reviews is the threshold where a profile stops
looking abandoned.

**Where to start.** The last twenty customers you finished a job for. They
already like you and they have forgotten to say so.

**How to ask.** In person is twice as effective as any message. At handover, when
they are happy with the result:

> "If you're happy with how it turned out, a Google review would really help
> us. I'll text you the link so it's there when you have a minute."

Then send the short review link from your profile. Do not make them search.

**What not to do**, all of which will cost you the profile:

- Do not pay for reviews or offer a discount for one.
- Do not write them yourself, or ask family to.
- Do not ask only the customers you think will be positive. Filtering who you ask
  is against Google's rules and, in Italy, against consumer protection law.
- Do not buy them. They are detected and removed, and the profile is penalized.

**Aim for a steady trickle.** Two or three a month, forever, beats thirty in one
week. A burst looks manufactured and gets filtered out.

**Reply to every review within a few days.** Positive ones get two sentences
naming the work: *"Thanks Sarah — good to work on the rewire in Chorlton."*
That reply is read by future customers, and the words in it tell Google what
you do and where.

Negative ones get a short factual reply and an offer to talk offline. Never
argue in public. A calm reply to a one-star review sells better than the five-star
ones above it.

## Week three: be findable everywhere else

One evening. Use the copy-pasted block from week one for every single entry.

| Where | Why | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Bing Places | Roughly a tenth of searches in most markets, and it feeds Microsoft Copilot | 20 min |
| Apple Business Connect | Every iPhone user asking Siri or Maps | 20 min |
| Your national directory | Yell, PagesJaunes, PagineGialle, Yelp — whichever leads your market | 20 min |
| Facebook page | A citation even if you never post again | 20 min |
| Your trade association | Real authority, topically relevant, almost nobody claims theirs | 30 min |
| Public company register | Companies House or equivalent; check it matches | 15 min |
| Supplier "find an installer" pages | The best links available to a trade, and free | 1 hour |

That last row is the one people skip and it is the most valuable. If you install
a manufacturer's products, they almost certainly have a page listing approved
installers, and you almost certainly have never asked to be on it. Call your
sales rep.

Do not pay anyone to submit you to two hundred directories. It does nothing, and
it creates two hundred places where your details will go out of date.

## The weekly rhythm — thirty minutes

Pick a fixed slot. Friday afternoon works because the week's jobs are fresh.

- **Photograph one job.** Before, during, after. Upload three to the profile.
  Three minutes.
- **Ask two customers for a review.** The two you finished this week.
- **Reply to any new review.**
- **Answer any message** on the profile or WhatsApp. An unanswered message is
  worse than not offering messaging at all.
- **Write down one question a customer asked you this week.** Keep a running list
  in your phone. That list becomes the content of your website, and it is worth
  more than anything a copywriter would invent, because it is what people
  actually want to know.

---

## The monthly rhythm — two hours

- **Check the numbers.** Google Business Profile insights: calls, direction
  requests, website clicks. Search Console: impressions and clicks. You are
  looking for direction, not precision.
- **Search for yourself.** From a phone, logged out, not at your office. Search
  your main service plus the city. Where do you appear in the map results? Note
  it. Do this from the same place every month or the numbers are not comparable.
- **Ask an assistant about you.** Type "who does [your service] in [your city]" into
  ChatGPT and Perplexity, and "cosa fa [your business name]". Two things matter:
  whether you appear at all, and whether what it says about you is *correct*. A
  confidently wrong statement about your prices or your area is worth reporting
  and worth fixing on your website.
- **Update anything that changed.** Hours, services, phone. In all the places
  from week three.
- **One outreach action.** One of:
  - Email a local news outlet with an actual story. Not "we exist" — something
    with a fact in it, or something useful before a seasonal problem.
  - Contact one partner business: an architect, an estate agent, a designer. Ask
    to be on their recommended-suppliers list, and offer the same.
  - Sponsor something small and local. A team kit, a school event. The mention on
    their website is a link from exactly the right kind of local domain.
  - Answer three questions in a forum or group where people ask about your trade,
    as a person who knows the answer, not as an advert.

## Twice a year — half a day

- **Check your details everywhere.** Search your phone number in quotes on
  Google. Every result should show the same name, address and phone. Fix the ones
  that drifted.
- **Look for mentions without links.** Search your business name in quotes. If
  someone wrote about you and did not link, email them and ask. It is the easiest
  link you will ever get.
- **Photograph the best job of the last six months properly.** Good light, tidy
  site, wide shots. These become the website's gallery.
- **Write down what changed in the business.** New service, new certification,
  new person, new area covered. Send it to whoever maintains the website. A
  website that describes a business from three years ago undersells you.

---

## What you should never do

- **Never pay for reviews, links, or "guaranteed first page".** All three are
  either penalized or fake. Anyone guaranteeing a Google position is guaranteeing
  something they do not control.
- **Never put keywords in your business name** on the profile.
- **Never create a second profile** for the same location.
- **Never list an address you cannot receive customers at.**
- **Never let someone else own your profile.** If an agency created it, get
  ownership transferred to your own Google account. This goes wrong constantly
  and businesses lose profiles they built over years.
- **Never let someone else own your domain.** Register it in your own name, with
  your own card. Same reason.

---

## What to expect, and when

So you can tell whether it is working rather than guessing.

- **Weeks 1-4.** The profile gets verified and starts appearing for searches with
  your business name. Not yet for your services.
- **Months 2-3.** With ten or more reviews and a complete profile, you start
  appearing in map results for specific searches — the long, specific ones first.
  Website pages get indexed and collect their first impressions.
- **Months 3-6.** More competitive searches begin to move. This is where steady
  review collection separates you from competitors who did it once and stopped.
- **Months 6-12.** Compounding. AI assistants start mentioning businesses that
  have accumulated real mentions, usually after they have become visible in
  ordinary search, not before.

If someone promised you page one in thirty days, they promised you a search for
your own business name.
